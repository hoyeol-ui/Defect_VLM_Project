## Study title

Human Annotation Time and Quality in Active Learning for Industrial Defect Detection

## Why this study is being conducted

Active Learning systems usually count the number of selected images as the annotation budget. Images can nevertheless require very different amounts of human work because they contain different numbers, sizes, and types of defects. This study measures how long it takes to annotate selected images, how complete and accurate the resulting boxes are, and whether those labels are useful for a later detector-learning study.

The study does not evaluate your employment performance. Results are intended to evaluate the data-selection procedures and measurement protocol.

## What you would do

- complete a short annotation-guide training;
- annotate a separate Stage 0 set used to test the software logger;
- if the study is authorized, draw and classify bounding boxes on a blinded image set;
- use pause/resume when taking a break;
- submit each image when finished;
- optionally participate in a separately explained disagreement review.

You will not be told which Active Learning strategy selected an image. Reference annotations are hidden during your work.

## What is recorded

- a pseudonymous annotator ID;
- session and task IDs;
- image start, pause, resume, and submit events;
- active and wall-clock durations;
- box creation, movement, resizing, deletion, and class changes;
- final annotations and any adjudication corrections;
- tool and protocol versions.

Your name and email must not be stored in annotation event logs. Screen recording is off unless separately approved and separately consented.

## Time, breaks, and discomfort

The expected number and length of sessions will be filled in after the protocol decisions are approved. Annotation may cause fatigue, eye strain, or repetitive-input discomfort. You may use the pause control and take the scheduled breaks. Contact the researcher if you experience discomfort.

## Voluntary participation and withdrawal

Participation is voluntary. Refusing or withdrawing must not affect grades, employment, supervision, or access to services. The final institution-approved form will explain the withdrawal deadline and whether already deidentified data can be removed.
